What dating apps
get wrong

Swipe apps are tuned for matches and messages. People join them to go on dates. Tender is built around the part in between.

The problemDating apps todayTender's bet

Every profile looks the same

Selfies, a job title and a two-line bio. Choices come down to one photo.

A cooking video shows effort, taste and personality at once. You see how he'd treat you at dinner.

Conversations die at "hey"

Matches sit unanswered because nobody knows how to start.

Every match opens with a dish she already rated. The first message writes itself.

Matches never turn into dates

Planning is left to the users, so the chat drifts and fades.

A built-in planner, restaurant picks and booking. The next step is always one tap away.

Low effort is rewarded

Mass swiping costs nothing, so women get flooded and men get ignored.

Posting a dish takes real effort. Ratings surface the men who actually show up.

Dinners,
not matches

Tender measures success by match-to-date rate: how many matches end with a booked table or a cooked meal. It's the number that matters to people, and the one most dating apps aren't designed around.

How Tender
makes money

Revenue comes from the meals the app creates, not from charging people to be seen.

Restaurant partners

Paid per seated table

Local restaurants join the curated list and pay when a Tender date is booked and seated. Couples who come back become regulars.
